I’d change the information hierarchy before coding. The ingredients make sense, but four equally prominent panels will compete for space—and several depend on the same selected pod.

The biggest improvements I’d make:

- **Make pods the primary view.** Namespace selection is usually occasional; the pod list is where users spend their time. Put namespaces in a picker or a collapsible sidebar.
- **Use one secondary pane for details or logs.** Let users switch between them and expand either to full screen. A four-panel layout could remain an optional wide-screen mode.
- **Summarize details first.** Start with status, readiness, restarts, node, IP, age, and owner. Put labels, annotations, conditions, and other metadata in expandable sections. Long annotations will otherwise dominate the panel.
- **Add events early.** When a pod is pending or failing, recent events and condition reasons often explain more than its metadata. Make them easy to reach from the selected pod.
- **Show a few contextual shortcuts.** Keep navigation, search, actions, and `? Help` visible; put all 14 bindings in a help overlay. Update hints when focus changes.
- **Design for smaller windows now.** At 160×50, the grid may look comfortable. Try sketches at 100×30 and 80×24, with explicit rules for collapsing panes and hiding columns.

For Kubernetes specifically, I’d also sharpen a few behaviors:

**Status needs more than three colors.** A pod can be `Running` while its containers are unready or restarting. Show readiness (`1/2`), restarts, and a useful reason such as `CrashLoopBackOff` alongside color. Keep the text readable without color.

**Logs need explicit scope and control.** Show the pod and container being tailed, support container selection and previous-container logs, and distinguish following from paused scrolling. If selecting another pod automatically changes the tail, make that behavior obvious; optionally let users pin it.

**Focus needs a strong visual signal.** Rounded borders are fine, but the active pane should stand out through its border and title, with a selected row that remains distinguishable when its pane loses focus. Define how keyboard focus moves before implementing the widgets.

A starting layout I’d favor:

```text
Context / cluster                    Namespace: payments
Pods                     Filter: /                     LIVE
NAME          READY  STATUS             RESTARTS    AGE
api-…         1/2    CrashLoopBackOff          7     2h
worker-…      1/1    Running                   0     1d
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Details | Events | Logs                 api-… / app
Selected pod summary or active tab content
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
/ Filter   Enter Expand   Tab Focus   ? Help
```

Before building widgets, sketch three workflows: finding an unhealthy pod, understanding why it is unhealthy, and reading the right container’s logs. If those flows feel quick at 100×30, you’ll have a strong foundation.
