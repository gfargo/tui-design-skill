I’d change a few things before coding. The biggest issue is that the layout gives equal space to four things with very different roles. Namespace is a filter, pods are the main navigation, and details and logs are inspection views. A fixed grid will make the pod list and logs feel cramped while leaving the namespace panel underused.

I’d start with this structure:

- **Header:** cluster/context and active namespace. Keep the context conspicuous, especially for production.
- **Main area:** a wide pod list, with an optional details or logs pane.
- **Namespace picker:** open it on demand as a searchable overlay, with an “all namespaces” option.
- **Footer:** a few actions relevant to the current view, plus `? Help` for the full binding list.

Specific changes I’d make:

**Make details a summary first.** Lead with status, readiness, restarts, age, node, IP, and owner. Put conditions and recent events near the top when something is wrong. Labels and annotations belong in expandable sections; annotations can consume an enormous amount of space. Offer a full YAML view for exhaustive inspection.

**Treat logs as a mode with room to breathe.** A quarter of the screen is tight for timestamps and long lines. Support expanding logs, choosing a container, viewing previous-container logs, searching, and toggling wrap. Clearly show whether the view is following live output or paused. Scrolling up should pause following without snapping the user back down.

**Define focus and selection behavior early.** Make the focused pane obvious beyond its rounded border. Show which pod the details and logs belong to. Preserve pod selection by identity as rows update, and avoid reordering the list under the cursor. Consider requiring an explicit action to start logs so navigating the pod list doesn’t constantly replace the stream.

**Use more than color for health.** Keep readable status text and show readiness, such as `1/2`, alongside restart counts. A pod can be `Running` while a container is crash-looping or failing readiness checks. Green should convey useful health, not just the pod phase.

**Design for smaller terminals now.** Your 160×50 window is a good spacious case, but try the design at 100×30 and 80×24. Collapse secondary panes, drop lower-priority columns, and let the active view fill the screen. Rounded borders are fine, but four bordered panes plus header and footer spend a lot of cells on structure.

Before implementation, sketch three tasks: finding an unhealthy pod, diagnosing why it won’t start, and reading one container’s logs. Include empty results, lost connections, and permission errors in those sketches.

My strongest recommendation: **make the pod list the stable center of the interface, and let inspection views expand around it.** That gives you a clearer hierarchy and a much easier path to a useful small-terminal experience.
