**I’d change the equal four-panel grid before coding.** Your likely loop is “choose namespace → find an unhealthy pod → inspect why → read logs.” Give the pod list most of the space, and make the other views support that loop.

1. **Make namespaces a picker or collapsible sidebar.** They usually change less often than pod selection. A permanent quarter-screen namespace list spends a lot of space on occasional navigation. Keep the active namespace and context visible in your header.

2. **Replace “every field” with a short diagnostic summary.** Start with readiness, status/reason, restarts, age, node, and owner. Put IPs and QoS in an expandable section; labels and annotations behind explicit expansion with counts. Long annotations can consume the entire panel. Add an Events view: for Pending pods, scheduling and image-pull events often explain more than logs.

3. **Give logs full width and an expand action.** Half-width logs wrap heavily, making stack traces difficult to follow. Always label the pod and container being streamed; support container selection and previous-container logs for crash loops.

**Define these behaviors before settling the visuals:**

- **Stable selection:** preserve the selected pod by UID as updates arrive. Don’t let sorting move the cursor onto another pod. If it disappears, show “pod deleted” before changing the inspected target.
- **Explicit log follow:** scrolling up pauses following; show `PAUSED · 128 new lines`, with a clear resume key. Incoming lines must not drag someone away from what they’re reading. Keep a bounded buffer.
- **Visible focus:** use a bold or marked pane title, plus optional accent color; reserve reverse video for the active row. `Tab`/`Shift+Tab` move between visible panes. Keep scrolling local to the focused pane.
- **Smaller help:** show roughly five contextual actions and `? help`; put all 14 bindings in help. `/` filters pods but searches within logs. `Esc` closes or backs out, `q` quits at the root, and `Ctrl+C` cancels or exits cleanly.

Your status colors are conventional, but **Running does not necessarily mean healthy**. Show `READY 0/1` and the actual reason alongside color. Distinguish Completed, Terminating, and Unknown too. Honor `NO_COLOR`; the interface should remain understandable in monochrome.

The clutter audit is fairly concrete:

- Border nesting is **one level**, which is reasonable; rounded corners themselves are fine.
- Assuming equal panes, one-row header/footer, and no gaps, four borders consume about **12% of the 160×50 screen**. At 80×24, that rises to about **22%**, before field labels or help text.
- Remove the namespace box by default and replace adjacent closed boxes with shared separators. Avoid repeating cluster/namespace in every title.
- Status text plus color is enough; extra badges and icons would repeat the same signal. Use a row marker only for selection.

I’d specify this resize ladder:

| Size | Behavior |
|---|---|
| 160×50 | Pod list, compact summary, full-width logs; optional namespace sidebar |
| 120×30 | Pod list plus one inspector view: Summary, Events, or Logs |
| 80×24 | Full-width pod list; details/logs open as separate views |
| 60 columns | One pane; keep name, readiness, status; truncate names and reveal the full value in details |
| Below a proposed 60×16 minimum | Show the required size and current size; preserve state until resized |

Also sketch loading, no pods, no filter matches, permission denied, and disconnected states. A frozen watch should say **“Disconnected · last update 32s ago”**, rather than look live; log failures should not blank the pod list.

For Ratatui, keep Kubernetes watches and log streaming off the rendering/input path. Before building out features, verify these layouts with `TestBackend` at the sizes above, including monochrome focus, and ensure terminal restoration on exit/error. Provide linear output for screen-reader and non-TTY use.
