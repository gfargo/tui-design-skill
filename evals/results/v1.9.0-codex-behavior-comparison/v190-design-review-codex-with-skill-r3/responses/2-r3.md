I’d change the layout before coding. **Make the pod list the primary workspace, with details and logs available on demand.** Your four quadrants give namespace selection as much space as the work users will spend most of their time doing.

The main loop to optimize is: choose scope → find a pod → understand its problem → inspect a container’s logs → return to the list.

1. **Replace the permanent namespace panel with a searchable picker.** Keep the active namespace in your header; `n` opens the picker, Enter commits, Esc cancels. An optional sidebar can serve users who switch namespaces constantly.

2. **Replace “every field” with a diagnostic summary.** Show readiness, status/reason, restarts, age, node, and owner first, plus unhealthy conditions and recent warning events. Put IPs and QoS in an expanded overview; labels and annotations in searchable sections; expose the complete object through a YAML view. Long annotations can otherwise consume the entire quadrant.

   Also, **Running doesn’t mean healthy**: a pod can be Running with containers unready. Show `READY 1/2` and the actual waiting/termination reason. Treat CrashLoopBackOff as a container reason rather than a pod phase.

3. **Give logs enough width and explicit ownership.** Open a wide log view with the pod name, container, and current/previous instance visible. Scrolling upward pauses following; show `PAUSED · 37 new`, with `G` to resume. Let users maximize it and control wrapping. Avoid silently switching the stream while they’re reading historical lines.

4. **Define focus and refresh behavior now.** Tab/Shift+Tab moves between visible panes; arrows and `j/k` navigate; `/` filters; Enter opens details; `l` opens logs. Mark focus through a visible title marker or border weight as well as color. Preserve selection by pod UID across updates; if it disappears, say so instead of silently targeting another pod. Esc closes a picker or returns from a view; `q` quits from the root.

5. **Shorten the footer and reduce decoration.** Show roughly five contextual actions plus `?` for the complete keymap. Fourteen bindings will compete with the content and fail at smaller sizes.

Your clutter audit, based on the description:

- **Border depth: one**, which is reasonable; rounded corners themselves aren’t the problem.
- Assuming equal quadrants, one-row header/footer, and no gutters, four panel borders consume **816 of 8,000 cells—10.2% of the screen**, before headings or padding.
- State currently has one specified cue: color. Use **status text plus color**, honoring `NO_COLOR`; avoid adding redundant badges and icons.
- No repeated row markers are specified. Keep any selection marker exclusive to the selected row.
- Remove the namespace box and separate details/log boxes; one inspector with tabs saves space and clarifies their relationship.

Use a deliberate resize policy:

| Size | Behavior |
|---|---|
| 120+ columns, sufficient height | Pod list and inspector; allow maximizing either |
| 80×24 | Full-width pod list; details/logs replace it on drill-in |
| 60-column split | Single pane; retain name, readiness, status; hide secondary columns and truncate names |
| Below a proposed 60×12 minimum | Show required dimensions; keep quit working |

Preserve the selected pod, filter, and scroll positions when changing layouts.

Before implementation, sketch loading, empty namespace, no filter matches, forbidden access, disconnected API, and ended log-stream states. Keep cached rows visible with `STALE` and their age during reconnects. For Ratatui, keep network work outside rendering, bound log retention, and verify terminal restoration on errors and panics. Plan a linear output mode for accessibility and automation.

**The biggest improvement is giving investigation more space:** fewer permanent panes, a useful summary, and a log view users can read without fighting it.
