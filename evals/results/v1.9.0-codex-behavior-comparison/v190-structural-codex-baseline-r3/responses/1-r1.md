I’d change four things before release, starting with behavior at smaller sizes.

1. **Add responsive layouts.** Wide monitors still get split terminals, larger fonts, and zoom. Keep three panes when space allows; collapse namespaces into a picker at medium widths; show resources and YAML as switchable views at narrow widths. Test at 80, 100, and 140 columns, plus short terminal heights.

2. **Reduce the framing.** Drop the double-line outer frame—it consumes space without separating useful content. Use subtle pane dividers and make keyboard focus obvious with an accent border or header. Keep selection visually distinct from focus.

3. **Make status meaningful.** Remove the leading bullets unless they convey state. A green `[READY]` and a checkmark repeat the same signal and draw attention to every healthy row. Use a compact, text-readable status column, with stronger emphasis for pending, failing, or unknown states. Use resource-specific status: a completed Job and a ready Deployment mean different things.

4. **Trim the shortcut footer.** Eighteen shortcuts across two rows are hard to scan. Show a few actions relevant to the focused pane, plus `? Help`. Put the complete, grouped shortcut list in a help overlay.

I’d also verify before shipping:

- Long names, empty namespaces, loading, permission errors, and disconnected clusters have usable layouts.
- Cluster/context and namespace scope remain visible, especially before destructive actions.
- YAML supports scrolling, search, and copying without silently truncating content.
- Selection survives refreshes, and status remains understandable without color.

The biggest release risk is assuming 140 columns. The biggest visual improvement is removing redundant borders and row decorations so selection, focus, and failures stand out.
